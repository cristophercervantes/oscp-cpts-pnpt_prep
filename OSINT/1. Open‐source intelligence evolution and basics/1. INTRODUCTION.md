# OSINT — Introduction

The internet didn't just connect people — it made information publicly accessible at a scale never seen before. That shift gave birth to **Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT)**: the practice of collecting, analyzing, and making decisions from publicly available data.

OSINT isn't one single method. It's a multi-method approach used across fields like national security, business intelligence (BI), and cybersecurity to extract useful insights, support informed decisions, and reduce risk.

## What Counts as "Open Source" Data?

OSINT pulls from a wide range of sources — both online and offline:

|Source Type|Examples|
|---|---|
|**Public Records**|Government docs, court records, regulatory filings|
|**Media & News**|Newspapers, broadcast news, online articles|
|**Social Media**|Facebook, Twitter/X, Instagram, YouTube|
|**Websites & Forums**|Blogs, forums, academic papers, research publications|
|**Commercial Data**|Market research reports, industry databases|

##  Principles of OSINT

Good OSINT isn't just about finding data — it's about finding it _responsibly and reliably_. Four principles guide practitioners:

- **Transparency** — Only use publicly available, legally obtained sources. No grey areas.
- **Relevance** — Stick to what actually matters for your objective. Don't collect noise.
- **Accuracy** — Verify before you trust. Misinformation kills the value of any analysis.
- **Legal & Ethical Compliance** — Respect privacy laws and individual data rights. This one's non-negotiable.

## Where OSINT Gets Used

OSINT has carved out a critical role in three major domains:

- **National Security** — Monitoring threats, tracking actors, understanding geopolitical movements
- **Business Intelligence** — Competitive research, market analysis, due diligence
- **Cybersecurity** — Threat intelligence, attack surface mapping, breach investigation

## Real-World Examples

These are the kinds of things OSINT actually looks like in practice:

- **Financial Investigations** — Reading between the lines of a company's annual reports and news coverage to understand their real financial situation
- **Digital Footprints** — Tracing someone's online presence across social media, public records, and digital directories
- **Political Pulse** — Gauging public sentiment and political climate through local news and social media trends
- **Disease Detectives** — Tracking disease spread by aggregating data from health websites and social media reports
- **Political Watchdogs** — Monitoring organizations by analyzing their online activity and public messaging

OSINT is a living, evolving discipline. As the flow of information grows faster and more complex, the ability to make sense of it — ethically and accurately — becomes more valuable. It's not just a skill for intelligence analysts anymore; it's relevant to journalists, security researchers, investigators, and anyone who needs to understand the world through publicly available data.
