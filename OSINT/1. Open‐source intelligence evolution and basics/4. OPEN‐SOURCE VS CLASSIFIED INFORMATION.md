# Open-Source vs Classified Information

Two completely different categories of data with different rules, access levels, and consequences.

## Open-Source Information (OSI)

Publicly available. Anyone can access it — no clearance, no special permission needed.

Sources include books, newspapers, websites, academic papers, social media, public records, magazines. It flows through legitimate, authorized channels.

traits:

- No classification or security markings
- No restrictions on who can use it
- Sources are visible and not concealed
- Legal to obtain and use
- Valuable for research, analysis, and decision-making

OSI is in the public domain by nature. Transparency is built into it.

## Classified Information

Sensitive data protected because its exposure could harm national security, diplomatic relations, or individuals' safety.

Access is tightly controlled — you need:

1. The right security clearance level
2. A legitimate need-to-know
3. Compliance with security protocols

Access is also **compartmentalized** — you only see what's relevant to your specific role, nothing more.

### Classification Levels (typical)

- **Confidential** — lowest, still restricted
- **Secret** — moderate sensitivity
- **Top Secret** — highest, most restricted

### How it's protected

- Secure physical storage
- Encryption
- Controlled access points
- Strict handling procedures

Violating these = criminal charges, penalties, or worse.

Classification isn't permanent — agencies review it periodically and declassify when the info is no longer sensitive enough to warrant protection.
