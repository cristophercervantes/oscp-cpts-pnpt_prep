# The Intelligence Lifecycle

OSINT work isn't random — it follows a structured cycle. Think of it like a contract job: a client needs intel on a target, and you move through defined stages to deliver it. The cycle never really "ends" — it feeds back into itself as new information surfaces.

## The 5 Stages at a Glance

```
Planning → Collection → Processing → Analysis → Dissemination
```

## Stage 1: Planning & Direction

Before you touch a single source, you need to know _what_ you're looking for and _why_.

**What happens here:**

- Define the intelligence requirement — what specific question are you answering?
- Identify the target — a person, group, organization, or location
- Build a collection plan — which sources, methods, and tools will you use?

**Things to think about when planning:**

- What's the target's digital footprint like?
- What public records are available?
- Are there human sources worth tapping?

**Example — monitoring a foreign terrorist org:**

- Social media monitoring (Facebook, Twitter, Telegram)
- Open-source forums where members might communicate
- Public records (court docs, property records, corporate filings)
- Human intelligence from contacts with insider knowledge

> The clearer your requirement upfront, the less noise you collect later.

## Stage 2: Collection

This is where most of the time goes. You're executing the plan and pulling data from every relevant source.

**Online sources:**

|Source|What you get|
|---|---|
|Social Media|Interests, affiliations, activity patterns, connections|
|News Articles|Key players, event timelines, different perspectives|
|Blogs & Forums|Niche discussions, insider opinions, community knowledge|
|Public Records|Verifiable facts — court docs, filings, property records|

**Other collection methods:**

- **Human Intelligence (HUMINT)** — interviews, contacting associates, expert consultations
- **Physical Surveillance** — observing locations or individuals in person
- **Technical Surveillance** — geolocation, communication interception (where legally permitted)

> Collection is systematic, not random. You follow the plan and document everything.

## Stage 3: Processing & Exploitation

Raw data is useless until it's cleaned up and made sense of. This stage is about turning collected information into something workable.

**Analysis techniques:**

- **Keyword Searching** — finding relevant info within large datasets
- **Network Analysis** — mapping who's connected to who
- **Time Series Analysis** — spotting trends and anomalies over time
- **Data Visualization** — charts, graphs, maps to make patterns visible

**Evaluating reliability — ask yourself:**

- Is the source credible? Any known biases?
- Does this information line up with other sources?
- What's the context — when and why was this shared?
- Can it be independently verified?

**Integrating with other intelligence types:**

- **HUMINT** — combine with info from human sources
- **SIGINT** — layer in intercepted communications data
- **MASINT** — add data from physical sensors (satellites, radar)

> This stage answers: _Is this data trustworthy, and what does it actually mean?_

## Stage 4: Analysis & Production

Now you take everything processed and turn it into something a decision-maker can actually use.

**Intelligence products you might produce:**

|Product|Purpose|
|---|---|
|**Report**|Full breakdown — methodology, sources, findings|
|**Briefing**|Short, punchy summary for busy stakeholders|
|**Alert**|Urgent notification of something requiring immediate action|
|**Visualization**|Maps, graphs, network diagrams to communicate complex data|

**Getting it to the right people:**

- Know your audience — tailor the product to what they need
- Choose the right delivery channel (email, secure messaging, in-person)
- Protect confidentiality — only share with who needs to know

> This stage answers: _What does this mean, and what should be done about it?_

## Stage 5: Dissemination

Delivering the intelligence product is only half of this stage. You also need to make sure stakeholders _understand_ it.

**Delivery involves:**

- Getting products to the right people at the right time
- Using appropriate channels — secure where needed
- Protecting the intelligence from unauthorized access

**Providing context means:**

- Explaining the implications of what was found
- Offering concrete recommendations for action
- Giving background info so findings make sense to non-analysts

**Example output for a terrorism investigation:**

- Detailed report on org structure and leadership
- Briefing on recent activities and threat level
- Alert if an imminent event is identified
- Map of the network's contacts and geographic spread

> Dissemination isn't just delivery — it's making sure the intelligence _lands_ and drives action.

## The Cycle Never Stops

After dissemination, new questions often emerge from the findings. Those questions feed back into Stage 1, and the cycle starts again. OSINT is ongoing work, not a one-time task.
