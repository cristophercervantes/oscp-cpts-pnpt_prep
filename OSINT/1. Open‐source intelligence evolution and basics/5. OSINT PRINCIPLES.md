# OSINT Principles

Three core principles run everything in OSINT: **transparency, legality, and relevance**. They're not independent — they overlap and reinforce each other constantly.

## Transparency

Be open about where your data comes from, how you got it, and what tools you used. Others should be able to independently verify your findings.

This means:

- Cite your sources explicitly — public records, social media, news, academic papers, whatever it is
- Disclose your methodology — tools, techniques, algorithms used
- Acknowledge limitations — publicly available info can be incomplete, inaccurate, or biased. Say so. Don't hide it.

Transparency builds trust and enables accountability. Without it, findings can't be scrutinized or validated.

## Legality

Just because information is public doesn't mean anything goes. Legal limits still apply.

Key areas to watch:

- **Privacy laws** — accessing or sharing someone's personal data without authorization is illegal in most jurisdictions
- **Copyright and IP** — you need the legal right to use and share content found in open sources. Reproducing or distributing copyrighted material without permission = legal exposure
- **Terms of service** — violating a platform's ToS can get accounts suspended, cut off valuable sources, or trigger legal action

Ethical data collection and legal compliance are the same thing in OSINT. Don't treat them separately.

## Relevance

Data for the sake of data is useless. Everything collected should serve the mission.

Three things relevance demands:

1. **Clear objectives** — define what you're trying to accomplish before you start collecting. National security? Competitive analysis? Cyber risk? The objective filters what matters.
2. **Signal over noise** — information overload is a real problem. Cut what doesn't contribute. Chasing irrelevant data wastes resources and kills focus.
3. **Timeliness** — old data loses relevance fast. Prioritize current information. Decisions should be based on what's accurate _now_, not last quarter.

## How the Three Principles Connect

They're interwoven, not separate:

- Transparency supports legality — disclosing methods shows you're operating within the law
- Relevance guides transparency — you disclose what matters to the mission, not everything
- Legality sets the floor for ethics — defines what data you can even touch
- Relevance supports legality — collecting only what's needed reduces the risk of accidentally crossing legal lines

## Ethical Landscape

The blurring of public and private info in the digital age makes ethics non-negotiable in OSINT. Ethics here isn't abstract — it directly affects trust, legality, and the integrity of your work.

Core ethical commitments:

- Respect platform terms of service and community guidelines
- Don't collect data you don't have the right to collect
- Balance the pursuit of knowledge against individual privacy rights

## Accuracy — Pursuing Truth

Bad data leads to bad decisions. Ethical OSINT practitioners treat accuracy as an obligation, not an aspiration.

- **Verify information** — cross-reference multiple sources, fact-check before using
- **Avoid misinformation** — never knowingly share false or unverified data. The consequences (reputational damage, panic, bad decisions) are real
- **Cite sources** — give credit, enable verification, create an audit trail

## Integrity & Accountability

Integrity means your work is honest, objective, and trustworthy. Accountability means owning the outcome of your work.

Integrity in practice:

- Hold to professional standards inside your org and with external stakeholders
- Keep analyses objective — no personal bias, no undue influence
- Protect sources — confidentiality and anonymity of sensitive sources must be respected

Accountability in practice:

- Own your mistakes — when errors are found, acknowledge them immediately and correct them
- Don't deflect or hide inaccuracies
