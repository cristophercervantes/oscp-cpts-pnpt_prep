# WHOIS

Query a domain and get back registration info — who owns it, who manages it, when it was registered, what nameservers it uses.

```bash
whois inlanefreight.com
```

What you get back:

- **Domain name** and registrar
- **Registrant / admin / technical contacts** — names, emails, phone numbers
- **Creation and expiration dates**
- **Name servers**

## Why it's useful for recon

- **People** — contact names and emails for social engineering or phishing targets
- **Infrastructure** — name servers and IPs hint at hosting setup and potential entry points
- **History** — services like WhoisFreaks show past records, so you can track ownership changes or old contact info that might still be valid

## Caveats

GDPR and privacy services have made a lot of WHOIS records less useful — personal info is often redacted now, replaced with the registrar's proxy details. Still worth running, but don't expect full contact info on every domain.

# Utilising WHOIS

```bash
sudo apt install whois -y
whois facebook.com
```

## What to look for in the output

- **Registration date** — very recent = suspicious (phishing domains are often registered days before a campaign)
- **Registrant** — hidden behind a privacy service? Red flag in the right context
- **Name servers** — who's actually hosting it? Known bulletproof hosting providers are a big tell
- **Domain status flags** — `clientTransferProhibited` etc. just means it's locked from being moved. Normal for legit domains.
- **Expiry date** — long expiry = established domain, short = throwaway

## What WHOIS actually catches in practice

**Phishing** — domain registered 3 days ago + hidden registrant + sketchy hosting provider = almost certainly malicious. Block it.

**Malware C2** — WHOIS on a C2 domain might reveal the hosting provider. Useful for reporting / takedown requests, and for pivoting to find other domains on the same infra.

**Threat intel** — when you track a threat actor across multiple domains, patterns emerge: same name servers, registration clusters right before attacks, shared registrars with lax abuse policies.
