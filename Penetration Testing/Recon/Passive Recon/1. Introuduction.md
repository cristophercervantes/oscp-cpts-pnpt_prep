# Web Reconnaissance

Recon is just information gathering before you do anything else. The more you know about a target, the better you can aim. There are two flavors: active and passive.

## Active Reconnaissance

You're directly touching the target. More information, higher risk of being noticed.

|Technique|What you're doing|Risk|
|---|---|---|
|Port scanning|Find open ports and services|High|
|Vulnerability scanning|Probe for known vulns|High|
|Network mapping|Map topology, hops, connected devices|Medium–High|
|Banner grabbing|Pull service banners to ID software/versions|Low|
|OS fingerprinting|Figure out what OS is running|Low|
|Service enumeration|Get exact version of services on open ports|Low|
|Web spidering|Crawl the site to map pages, dirs, files|Low–Medium|

Tools: Nmap, Nessus, Nikto, Burp Suite, Masscan

## Passive Reconnaissance

No direct contact with the target. Slower but stealthy — you're just reading publicly available stuff.

|Technique|What you're doing|Risk|
|---|---|---|
|Search engine queries|Google dorking, finding exposed info|Very low|
|WHOIS lookups|Domain registration info, registrant details|Very low|
|DNS analysis|Subdomains, mail servers, infrastructure|Very low|
|Web archive analysis|Old versions of the site via Wayback Machine|Very low|
|Social media analysis|Employee info, org structure, SE targets|Very low|
|Code repositories|GitHub leaks — credentials, keys, vulns|Very low|

Tools: whois, dig, dnsenum, Shodan, Wayback Machine, GitHub
